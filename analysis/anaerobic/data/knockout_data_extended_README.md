# knockout_data_extended.tsv

Physiology of *S. cerevisiae* deletion strains and their same-paper references, grown anaerobically (or quasi-anaerobically) on glucose. The values are transcribed from open-access full texts. There is one row per strain × quantity. `value` and `sd` are copied exactly as printed, including entries such as `<0.05`, `n.d.`, `ND` and `>150`. `source_type` shows whether a value comes from a table, from the text, or was read off a figure (`figure-estimate`). Attenuated-promoter alleles are described in `genotype` and `notes`; they are not listed in `deleted_genes_systematic`. The rows for Bakker 2000 (*adh3*) and Boles 1998 (*mae1*) stay in `knockout_data.tsv`.

**Papapetridis et al. 2016, Microb Cell Fact 15:67 (PMC5574463).** CEN.PK background. The reference is IMX585 (CEN.PK113-7D *can1::cas9-natNT2*), compared with IMX899 (IMX585 *ald6Δ*). Cultures were anaerobic batches in 2-L Applikon bioreactors (1-L working volume), sparged with N2 containing <10 ppm O2, with Norprene tubing and Viton O-rings. Temperature was 30 °C, stirring 800 rpm, and pH was held at 5.0 with KOH. The medium was Verduyn synthetic medium with ammonium sulfate as nitrogen source and 20 g/L glucose. It was supplemented with ergosterol (10 mg/L) and Tween 80 (420 mg/L), plus 0.2 g/L antifoam C. Rates and yields come from the exponential phase. The ethanol yield is corrected for evaporation. Biomass was estimated from OD660 calibration, and the ± values are mean deviations of duplicates. Table 4 gives no specific uptake or production rates (q). Strains with heterologous genes (gnd::gndA, gpd2::eutE) and the Table 5 cultures with acetate were left out.

**Hubmann et al. 2011, Appl Environ Microbiol 77:5857 (PMC3165387).** CEN.PK113-7D (MATa) is the wild type. The *gpd1Δ* strain IA1 comes from CEN.PK113-1A (MATα), and *gpd2Δ* (CE1) and *gpd1Δ gpd2Δ* (CE1.01) come from 113-7D. Cultures were grown under **quasi-anaerobic** conditions: 150 mL in 250-mL flasks with air locks, inoculated at OD600 1 from an aerobic preculture. The medium keeps its dissolved oxygen at the start, so the first phase is not anoxic. There was no pH control, 30 °C, and magnetic stirring at 200 rpm. The synthetic minimal medium contained 3 g/L (NH4)2SO4, vitamins and **50 g/L glucose**, with **no ergosterol or Tween 80**. Growth therefore depends on sterols and UFAs made during the initial oxygen phase and carried over from the preculture. Table 3 gives C-mol yields at the end of fermentation. CO2 was calculated from the weight loss, and no SD is printed. Also included are the "group II" strains that combine one deletion with a TEF1-mutant promoter on the other isogene (a7b, a2b, ab7, ab2). The Table 4 SSF results on wheat liquefact at pH 4.5 are included with their different medium noted. Group III strains (promoter swaps only, no deletions) were left out. **The paper gives no growth rates or specific rates.** GPDH activities appear only in Fig. 3A and were not extracted.

**Pagliardini et al. 2013, Microb Cell Fact 12:29 (PMC3639890).** CEN.PK113-7D wild type compared with *gpd2Δ* strains in which the GPD1 promoter is replaced by TEFmut7 or TEFmut2. The Results text gives residual GPDH activity of 55 % (0.023 U/mg) for TEFmut7 and 6 % (0.006 U/mg) for TEFmut2. These two %/U pairs are not consistent with a single wild-type activity. Cultures were run in the anaerobic "VHEP" fed-batch process in 5-L B. Braun reactors (3 L at start). The reactors were flushed with N2 at 0.5 L/min, and pH was held at 4 with 14 % NH3, so ammonia also serves as a nitrogen source. Temperature was 30 °C and vitamins were fed sequentially. The mineral medium contains 3 g/L (NH4)2SO4 **and 1 g/L sodium glutamate**. It was supplemented with ergosterol (63 mg/L) and Tween 80 (2.63 g/L), both dissolved in ethanol, and that ethanol was subtracted from the product totals. Glucose started at 100 g/L and was re-fed to 100 g/L whenever it fell below 20 g/L. Ethanol reached 57–139 g/L, which inhibits growth. Yields for biomass, glycerol and glycerol per biomass are for the growth windows printed in the table (WT 0–16 h, TEFmut7 0–30 h, TEFmut2 0–44 h). Y_ethanol is for the growth phase and Y_ethanol_overall covers the whole run. q rates, Y_ATP and ATP production rates come only from the authors' MFA model and are not in the table.

**Tai et al. 2007, Microbiology 153:877 (PMC2895221).** CEN.PK113-7D background with kanMX barcoded deletions. The deleted genes are listed in Supplementary Table S1 and Fig. 1. Relative fitness comes from an anaerobic glucose-limited **competitive chemostat** at D = 0.10 1/h, pH 5, 30 °C, N2 sparged, running for 216 h. The medium was Verduyn 1992 synthetic medium with Tween 80 and ergosterol, but the glucose concentration of the anaerobic chemostat is not stated. Fitness is expressed as the percentage reduction relative to the tagged neutral reference *ygr059wΔ::downtag*. **Per-strain fitness values are only shown as a bar chart (Fig. 3C).** They were read from the low-resolution published image (472×347 px) with an uncertainty of about ±2 %, and are marked `figure-estimate`. The five significant hits are *plb2*, *izh2*, *ylr413w*, *eug1* and *yor012w* (P<0.01). The *ura3Δ* strain is the auxotrophic negative control and washes out. Anaerobic μmax is printed (Table 1) **only for those five mutants plus *ygr059wΔ* and CEN.PK113-7D**, not for all 27 strains. These μmax values come from anaerobic batch bioreactors with 20 g/L glucose, Tween 80 and ergosterol. The *yor012wΔ* deletion also inactivates the overlapping ORF YOR013W. Table 1 also has an aerobic fitness column and aerobic shake-flask μmax, which were left out.

**Dekker et al. 2019, FEMS Yeast Res 19:foz060 (PMC6750169).** This paper tests whether yeast can grow anaerobically without unsaturated fatty acids (UFAs). **It prints no growth rates or yields for *ole1Δ*.** Two sets of rows are included. (1) Table 4 gives μ (from CO2 profiles), Y_ethanol and Y_biomass for **CEN.PK113-7D only**, in strictly anaerobic sequential-batch bioreactors. These used SMD-urea (urea as nitrogen source, no pH control) under N2 with ≤0.5 ppm O2, Fluran tubing and 0.2 bar overpressure. The carry-over cycle had no ergosterol or Tween 80, and cycles 1–4 had ergosterol only (10 mg/L) with no UFA. The glucose concentration is taken as 20 g/L (SMD) because it is not restated for the SBR. (2) Chamber experiments with IMX585 (reference) and IMK861 (*ole1Δ*) were run in an anaerobic chamber (85 % N2, 10 % CO2, 5 % H2, Pd catalyst). These used 40 mL SMD-urea shake flasks, with 50 g/L glucose in the carry-over culture and 20 g/L afterwards. Conditions were Tween 80 + ergosterol, ergosterol only (3 serial transfers), or neither. Residual glucose comes from Table S1. Maximum OD600 was read from Fig. 3, a single representative experiment. Deleting OLE1 did not impair anaerobic growth with or without Tween 80. Neither strain grew without ergosterol after the carry-over culture. Fatty-acid composition (Table S2) was not transcribed.

**Björkqvist et al. 1997, Appl Environ Microbiol 63:128 (PMC168310): not extracted.** PMC holds only a scanned PDF. The download sits behind a browser proof-of-work challenge, and the ASM and Europe PMC render pages block automated access. The abstract describes aerobic batch cultures on semisynthetic medium with 20 g/L glucose, where the parent and the *gpd1Δ* and *gpd2Δ* strains all have μ ≈ 0.5 1/h and *gpd1Δ gpd2Δ* grows about half as fast. The anaerobic data are aerobic-to-anaerobic step changes reported as CO2 evolution rates. After the switch, *gpd2Δ* CER fell by more than 50 % and partly recovered after about 45 min, and *gpd1Δ gpd2Δ* collapsed until acetoin was added. There is no anaerobic steady-state data in the abstract. The full text would have to be read by hand from the PDF.

**Påhlman et al. 2001, J Biol Chem 276:3555 (doi 10.1074/jbc.M007164200): not extracted.** The paper is free to read, but jbc.org, ScienceDirect and the Elsevier API all return Cloudflare or 403 pages to automated requests, and there is no PMC copy. The abstract gives only qualitative results: *gpp1Δ* strains grow poorly anaerobically, *gpp1Δ gpp2Δ* anaerobic growth is restored by acetaldehyde, and G3P accumulates. It needs manual retrieval.

**Other open datasets checked.** Guadalupe Medina et al. 2010 (AEM 76:190, PMC2798634) was left out. Its only anaerobic quantitative data are for the GPD1 GPD2 empty-vector reference (IME076) and the *gpd1Δ gpd2Δ* + *E. coli mhpF* strain on glucose + acetate, so it has no native-deletion-only strain. Guadalupe-Medina 2014 (PMC3896938) and van Aalst 2023 (PMC10647013) are likewise engineered strains. The following were not open-access or had no retrievable full text: Nissen et al. 2000 Yeast 16:463 (*gpd1/gpd2* anaerobic batch), Saint-Prix et al. 2004 Microbiology 150:2209 (*ald* deletions, anaerobic glucose, Dequin group), and Ansell et al. 1997 EMBO J (PMC1169820, scanned only). All three would be valuable additions if obtained manually.

**Additions from locally supplied full texts.** The paragraphs below cover eight papers transcribed from PDFs and text versions in `papers/`. That includes Nissen 2000, Saint-Prix 2004 and Ansell 1997, which are listed above as not retrieved. Tables were read from the text files and checked against rendered PDF pages. Figure values come from the rendered pages and are marked `figure-estimate`. In these rows, `~` marks a figure reading. FRDS/FRDS1 is FRD1 (YEL047C).

**Nissen et al. 2000, Yeast 16:463.** The strains are prototrophic haploids. TN1 is the wild type; according to the companion paper (Nissen et al. 2000, Metab Eng 2:69) it derives from CBS8066, which this paper does not state. TN4 is *gpd1Δ*, TN5 *gpd2Δ*, TN6 *gpd1Δ gpd2Δ*, and TN23 is TN6 *ura3Δ* carrying YEp24-PGKp-*cth* (the *A. vinelandii* transhydrogenase). All deletions are kanMX loop-outs. Cultures were anaerobic batches in 4.5-L in-house bioreactors, sparged with 0.4 L/min N2 containing <5 ppm O2, with Norprene tubing, pH 5.00 (KOH), 30 °C and 600 rpm. The medium was Verduyn mineral medium with 25 g/L glucose and 3.75 g/L (NH4)2SO4. Ergosterol (4.2 mg/g DW) and Tween 80 (175 mg/g DW) were added, dissolved in ethanol. The inoculum was 1 mg/L. Table 1 gives μmax and GPDH activity, and Table 2 gives yields in C-mol/C-mol. The paper prints no SDs and no q rates. TN5's carbon balance closes 4 % short; the authors attribute this to evaporated ethanol, and their corrected ethanol yield (0.514) is included as `Y_ethanol_corrected`. TN6 and TN23 did not grow anaerobically. The aerobic Table 3 (20 g/L glucose for TN1/4/5) was left out.

**Valadi et al. 1998, Appl Microbiol Biotechnol 50:434.** The background is W303-1A (*ade2-1 his3-11 leu2-3,112 trp1-1 ura3-1 can1-100*). The mutants are *gpd1Δ::TRP1* and *gpd2Δ::URA3*, so each strain has a different auxotrophy set. Cultures were anaerobic batches in a 2.5-L Belach fermentor flushed with N2 at 37.5 L/h, at pH 5.0 (NaOH), 30 °C and 400 rpm. The medium was defined CBS medium (Albers et al. 1996) with 7.5 g/L (NH4)2SO4 and 20 g/L glucose. Required bases and amino acids were added at 120 mg/L (leucine 240 mg/L), and these also act as extra carbon sources. **Ergosterol and Tween 80 are not mentioned.** Biomass was measured as dry weight. Transcribed are μ, specific and volumetric ethanol production (Table 1), all Table 2 yields, Y_ATP, heat yield and carbon balance, and protein content from the text. The gpd1Δ Table 2 column has no SD. Fig. 1 time courses and the Table 3 NADH balance were left out.

**Saint-Prix et al. 2004, Microbiology 150:2209.** There are two backgrounds. V5 (MATa ura3) is a haploid segregant of a Champagne wine strain; it has two copies each of ALD6, ALD4 and ALD5, and all copies were deleted. CEN.PK2-1C (MATa ura3-52 leu2-3,112 trp1-289 his3Δ MAL2-8c SUC2) is the laboratory strain. The null mutant *ald0Δ* is a CEN.PK2-1C × CEN.PK2-1D spore. Deletions are loxP-kanMX-loxP with marker recycling, and ALD2 and ALD3 were removed together as one tandem *ald3-2Δ* allele. Fermenters held 0.25 L, filled to 80 %, and were sealed with a cork with a CO2 vent tube. They were **not sparged**, so the conditions are semi-anaerobic with traces of O2 at the start. The authors state that V5 mutants behaved the same under strict anaerobiosis (data not shown). Temperature was 28 °C, stirring 500 rpm, and the inoculum 1×10^6 cells/mL. There were three media. YPD had 50 g/L glucose. MS synthetic must had 200 g/L glucose for V5 and 150 g/L for CEN.PK; it contains 6 g/L malic and 6 g/L citric acid, 300 mg N/L (180 mg N as amino acids and 120 mg N as NH4Cl) and is at pH 3.3, with methionine and uracil (V5) or the required amino acids (CEN.PK) added. All media had ergosterol 7.5 mg/L, oleic acid 2.5 mg/L and Tween 80 0.21 g/L. **Only acetate was measured**, as final concentration after glucose exhaustion (Table 3, single mutants). The paper reports no glycerol, ethanol, biomass yields or growth rates. Single mutants are described only qualitatively ("growth rate and final biomass similar to wild type", data not shown). Double, triple, quadruple and null-mutant acetate and maximum OD660 were read from Figs 2, 4 and 5b. The Fig. 5b legend does not name the medium; MS 15 % is inferred from the wild-type acetate level. ACDH activities come from the text. The Fig. 1 expression time courses were left out.

**Camarasa et al. 2003, Microbiology 149:2669.** The mutants are V5 *sdh1Δ* and *kgd1Δ* (kanMX4) in the V5 background (MATa ura3, wine-derived). The NMR strain K1 (a commercial wine strain) and its 13C resting-cell incubations were left out: they are cell suspensions without a nitrogen source, not growing deletion mutants. Fermenters held 1.1 L with fermentation locks and are described as "oxygen limitation". They were not sparged, ran at 28 °C and 500 rpm, and were inoculated at 1×10^6 cells/mL. All media had 200 g/L glucose, 6 g/L malic and 6 g/L citric acid, pH 3.5 and 300 mg N/L, and differ only in the **nitrogen source**. **MS** has mixed amino acids plus NH4Cl. **MA** has **aspartate** as sole N source. **MG** has **glutamate** as sole N source. The `cultivation` field records which medium each row used. **This paper does not state anaerobic growth factors**; the Dequin group's MS normally includes ergosterol, oleic acid and Tween 80. Table 2 gives end-of-fermentation concentrations in mM (ethanol in M) of succinate, 2-oxoglutarate, hydroxyglutarate, glycerol, acetate, pyruvate and ethanol for V5 and V5 *kgd1* on MA, MG and MS. The decimal points are garbled in the text extraction. The values were checked against the rendered page and the percentages in the text (kgd1 succinate is 60 % of the parent on MA, 28 % on MG and 75 % on MS). **The sdh1 metabolite data were not shown** ("no significant difference"), so sdh1 has only qualitative rows. OGDH activity and intracellular 2-OG come from the text. The *osm1 frds* no-growth statement is cited as unpublished data. No growth rates or biomass yields are given.

**Ansell et al. 1997, EMBO J 16:2179.** The strains are an isogenic W303-1A tetrad, YSH6.142-3A to 3D (wild type, *gpd1Δ::TRP1*, *gpd2Δ::URA3*, and both). Cultures were grown in rubber-stoppered serum flasks or tubes. The medium was flushed for 3 min with N2 (<5 ppm O2) and cultures were shaken at 30 °C. The medium was YNB without amino acids, with 2 % glucose, 120 mg/L of required nutrients, ergosterol 10 mg/L and Tween 80 420 mg/L. YP + 2 % glucose was used as the rich medium. The paper prints **no numbers** for anaerobic growth. The rows are qualitative phenotypes, plus μ and final OD610 read roughly from the scanned log-scale Fig. 1B (uncertainty about ±30 %). The glycerol amounts in Fig. 2 (4-h aerobic-to-anaerobic transfers) and the NAD(P)H levels in Fig. 3 were not transcribed. Only the statement that the double mutant produced no detectable glycerol is included.

**Arikawa et al. 1998, FEMS Microbiol Lett 165:111.** The background is DBY747 (MATa his3-Δ1 leu2-3,112 trp1-289). The control DURA is DBY747 with URA3 integrated. The disruptants are DFRDS (*frds::URA3*), DOSM (*osm1::URA3*) and DFRDOSM (*frds::URA3 osm1::THAr*). Anaerobic cultures were **static tubes overlaid with liquid paraffin**, with no gassing, which makes them semi-anaerobic. The medium was YPD with 2 % or 27 % glucose, plus ergosterol 12 mg/L and **no Tween 80**, at 30 °C. The rows are qualitative phenotypes from the text and maximum OD660 read from Fig. 3B/C. In the figure, DOSM stays near zero until aeration at 90 h, although the text says it "grew slowly". Fumarate reductase activities (Table 1) come from aerobic YPD10 cultures and were left out.

**Enomoto et al. 2002, FEMS Microbiol Lett 215:103.** The strains are DBY747 (ura3-52) and the Arikawa 1998 disruptants. The medium was chemically defined: 0.125 % YNB without amino acids or (NH4)2SO4, 5 % glucose, 1.25 % (NH4)2SO4, the required amino acids, ergosterol 25 mg/L and Tween 80 1.05 g/L, at 30 °C. The anaerobic liquid-culture method is only cited ("as described previously"). Plates were incubated in an anaerobic chamber with AnaeroPack. Table 2 gives stationary-phase (70 h) succinate with and without 0.08 mM methylene blue. DFRDOSM without methylene blue is printed as a dash. The text says DFRDOSM succinate is 21 % of DBY747, which does not match the printed values. Also included are qualitative plate phenotypes and DFRDOSM maximum OD660 against methylene blue concentration (Fig. 2A). The paper gives no growth rates, yields or by-products other than succinate.

**Camarasa et al. 2007, Yeast 24:391.** The strains are V5 *frds1Δ*, *osm1Δ* and *osm1Δ frds1Δ* (kanMX4, SFH/Cre-lox), plus the V5 *gpd1 gpd2* strain of Remize et al. 2003, which is used only as the acetoin control. **Strict anaerobiosis** was achieved in 300-mL fermenters (270 mL working volume): the medium was bubbled with argon for 15 min at 95 °C and the vessel was kept hermetically closed, with resazurin as indicator. Temperature was 28 °C and stirring 500 rpm. The oxygen-limited variant (Table 2) starts with traces of O2. MS had 200 g/L glucose and 460 mg N/L (340 mg N as amino acids and 120 mg N as NH4Cl) at pH 3.5. MG has glutamate as the sole N source. Both had ergosterol 7.5 mg/L, oleic acid 2.5 mg/L and Tween 80 0.21 g/L. Transcribed are the final succinate of the wild type and single mutants (text), the Table 2 final OD660 and intracellular succinate, and qualitative rescue results (O2, menadione, acetoin). The Northern, lacZ and fumarate reductase activity data were left out.
